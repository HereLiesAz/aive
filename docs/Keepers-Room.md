# The Keeper's room

Words: a **swarm** is a workflow (the saved plan: the queen, her creatures and their strands), a **hive** is the goal you want achieved, and a **run** is one execution of a swarm. This page says "hive" for the place where work happens (the Hive) and for the goal, and "swarm" for the saved workflow; the table is in [Vocabulary](Vocabulary.md).

The Aive has two places: **the hive**, where all work happens (`Swarm-OS.md`), and **the Keeper's room**, where everything that sets the hive up lives. Consolidating the app into those two places is planned in stages, below; all three are done.

## Parts

The room lists **six parts**, each with one sentence on the phone list (and as its heading on a wide window). They only regroup the earlier parts, whose sections below keep their names and contents; nothing is new.

| Part | One sentence | Holds (the earlier parts) |
|---|---|---|
| **Sources and tools** | What your creatures think with, the tools they may use, and where their work lands. | **Connections** (AI sources, remote A2A agents, repository hosts, tools), **Local agents** |
| **Allowance** | The most real money a run, or an hour, may spend. | **Limits**: the cost cap per run and the hourly spend cap, now on every width |
| **Hosts** | Where your creatures run: this device, your other machines, and models that run here. | **This device** (remote access, the A2A server, the local planner, local orchestration specialists, small tasks), **Compute** (only where the build offers distributed compute) |
| **Data and privacy** | Save, load, move or delete what The Aive keeps, and what it may report. | **Projects and data**, **About and privacy** |
| **Routines and trust** | Swarms that start by themselves, what your creatures may do without asking you, and what they suggest. | **Schedules**, **Delegations**, **Improvement** |
| **Look** | The colour behind your creatures. | **Appearance** |

Sources and tools and Hosts are listed under "Feeds the hive", the rest under "The keeper". The names are the owner's recommended defaults (decision D12; Compute is renamed Hosts per D31). The room remembers the open part by its first section (`keeper.section`); the breadcrumb names the part (`KeeperGroup`).

Compute shows only where the build offers distributed compute.

## Remembered text

Text fields start with something useful instead of empty. The first launch's goal starts with the last goal you fed, or with a starting goal for the linked repository (a fixed template, no model is asked); its repository starts with the last one you used for that kind of source. The launch bar, the feed bar, a continuation, the swarm prompt, the hive name and a role's standing instructions offer what you typed there before, in the same dropdown the repository search uses. A finished swarm's feed bar starts with its own goal; a running one starts empty. A step that failed, escalated or is blocked starts its message to the agent with "Retry with: <why it stopped>", and a rerun offers that line in its objective menu. A suggestion is selected, so typing replaces it, and one you did not edit is never saved as a draft. **About and privacy** has the switch (on by default; turning it off deletes what was remembered) and **Clear remembered text**. Up to 12 entries per kind stay on this device and are never sent or exported; key-like text is never kept, and key, token, pairing-code, header, passphrase, memory import/export and script fields are never filled in. See [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md).

## Delegations

When you approve the same creature's plan on the same source several times in a row (5 by default) and never reject it, a dialog asks once: "Let it proceed without asking for plan approval?". **Let it proceed** delegates that gate; **Keep asking** leaves it for the rest of the streak. A delegated gate approves itself and its run's events show "Approved by delegation". Only a creature that cannot write to a repository, deploy or write anywhere else, on a source that cannot either, is ever offered; `HumanApproval` tasks and every other gate always ask (`docs/THREAT_MODEL.md`, Progressive delegation). A rejection starts the streak over and ends the delegation. The Delegations part lists each one with **Revoke**. Delegations stay on this device and are never in the setup file or an export.

Memory is not here: it is a swarm of its own, running beside every other, so it lives in the hive: **Memory** is a view in the row of a live hive's Story (Overview, Waterfall, Events, Memory, Log) and holds the whole Memory swarm screen in place (`RunTimeline(memoryContent = …)`); on a wide window the rail's Memory place opens the same screen as a place of its own. Its stages that run without a model wear the neuron (`Node-Creatures.md`, Programmatic work).

## Connections

One row per thing (`ConnectionRow`): its name, one line about it, its status and what can be done to it. Connected things are Ink rows; things not yet connected are outlined. **Keyless sources start on.** The first time the app starts, every keyless source (Kilo Gateway, LLM7, OVHcloud AI Endpoints, the AI Horde image generator) is connected with no tap, once each: one you disconnect later stays off, and one a later release adds is turned on once. One **Keyless sources: on / off** switch at the top of Connections turns all of them off (or back on); each source also has its own **Disconnect**. A single line under the switch says that prompts and repository text sent to a keyless source leave this device and may be logged, and that these sources limit requests (Kilo 200 an hour, OVHcloud 2 a minute). Keyless sources serve work only when no source you added can, and never a creature set to another source. Ollama Cloud needs an account key (and credits for its larger models from 2026-11-09), so it is not one of them. While no AI source from the catalog is connected (an agent that comes with a repository host, such as OpenCode through GitHub, does not count; this happens after the switch is turned off), the same switch turns them back on (the per-gateway **Try it free, no key** card was folded into it: one route, not two). AI sources not yet connected sit behind one **Add a source** row rather than a card each, keyless ones first; those have no **Get API key**. On desktop a keyless link holds no secret and is stored without the keychain, so it works on Linux without `secret-tool`. Local Git needs no account: a folder is picked when launching. **Connect remote agent** reads and validates an external A2A Agent Card before adding that independent service as a source. The same form offers **Search directory**: an explicit remote lookup of independent agent listings by name or capabilities, combining local saved connections and suggested agents with a public A2A Registry (see `architecture/AGENT_DIRECTORY.md`). Search alone neither reads candidate Agent Cards nor creates a source. **Use address** fills the ordinary connection form, where **Connect agent** validates the actual Agent Card. Before searching, two optional public examples appear with **Use address** and locally persisted **Dismiss**. A bearer token is optional, and only roles explicitly assigned to the connected source send it tasks (`A2A.md`). Azphalt role/workflow/model packages are installed in the Store, not here. The old district hive-edge marketplace duplicated both surfaces and has been removed.
**Look for orphans** (the Search directory button) consults SearchA2A's public index, shared with Azphalt Store, and semantically ranks capabilities when inference is available. Aive falls back to SearchA2A's published keyword-searchable catalog if the gateway is unavailable. Both routes return untrusted metadata; selecting a result still requires separate Agent Card validation and an explicit source connection.

## This device

**A2A server** (desktop) is on unless you turn it off; the switch is kept once pressed. It listens only on this computer, shows its address, and has **Copy token** and **New token**. If another program holds its port, the row says so and the rest of The Aive works as usual. Under it, **Open to this network** (off by default) lets other computers reach the server; the warning beside it says traffic is not encrypted. While on, the row lists the Agent Card URL on each network address, shows each computer that tried with the token as "wants to send work" with **Allow**, and lists allowed computers with **Remove** (`A2A.md`). **Describe my sources in the card** lists which kinds of source are linked, and each role's source, on the public Agent Card (names and capabilities only). Until pressed it is on while the server is this computer only and off while open to the network; once pressed it stays as set. Swarm cards (**Share swarm card**, **Import swarm card**) are described in `A2A.md`.

**Small tasks on this device** (desktop and Android, off by default) sends short text tasks that need no repository access to the instruct model the memory clerks installed (`OnDeviceTextAgentProvider`, docs/Orchestration_layer.md, Agent Router). It downloads nothing: until a memory stage's local model on a shared base is installed from the Memory swarm, the switch is kept but nothing goes to the device. The choice is stored per device (`on-device.small-tasks.enabled`).
**MCP servers** follow, one row each with **Check**, **Always allow** or **Ask each run**, and **Remove**. **Add an MCP server** takes an address and an optional bearer token, or, on desktop, a command. Until a server is always allowed, the first call to it in each run asks the keeper (`docs/architecture/MCP_HOST.md`). On desktop, **Script sandbox** (off by default) lets agents call these tools from one short sandboxed script and receive only its result; the approvals still apply.

## Appearance

**Ground** is one radio group of six swatches, the current one ringed (`docs/BRANDING.md`, Ground and the shell header). **Reduce motion** (off by default, stored on this device as `aive.appearance.reduce-motion.v1`) holds the creatures and other looping movement still. It is OR-ed with the platform's own signal (Android's animator scale at 0, the browser's `prefers-reduced-motion`, or `AIVE_REDUCE_MOTION` on desktop), so it can turn motion off but never back on against the system; when the device already asks for less motion, the part says so.

**Creature labels** (off by default, stored on this device as `aive.appearance.always-show-creature-labels.v1`): a hive of more than 12 creatures normally shows only the selected creature's label so packed cells stay readable; turned on, every creature keeps its label however full the hive is (`docs/Node-Creatures.md`).

## Navigation

On a phone there is no navigation row. One header sits over every screen (`ShellHeader` in `ControlRoomUi.kt`):

- **The Aive** and the hive's state in a word: NEW, RESTING, WAKING, STOPPED, CAN'T REACH ITS AGENTS, or its run's status.
- **The breadcrumb**: plain text, Hive › source: repository/folder › hive name › creature › its tab, or Hive › a place opened from it (Roster, the Keeper's room › its part). The repository step names GitHub, GitLab or Local Git and is shown only when the open run has a recorded repository or its project has a linked one. It uses the run's repository snapshot when available. Objective-created hives show their project's name instead of the auto-generated 80-character prompt in the breadcrumb. The last step is where you are and is not a button; any earlier step goes back up to it, popping rather than stacking. Hive is the district, every hive at once; from a place opened off the hive, Hive returns to the hive you came from. A creature's step closes its panel. It replaces the page titles. On a phone it takes its own line and scrolls to keep where you are in view.
- **The Messages** badge and **the Keeper's room** gear.

Once there is a hive, resting or live, one row holds its places: its Roster and the Store (the Memory swarm is a view of a live hive's Story row). It is hidden on a first launch and while the hive is waking or stopped. The Store's tabs are **All**, **Agents**, **Hives**, **Breeds**, **Installed**, **Models** and **LLMs**. On wide screens (820dp and up) its tabs sit on an Ink capsule, the first hive the Store lists leads in a large card (**View hive**), and every package is a card (its creature on an art panel of its own hue, what it is, its name, a line, its version or install state, and **Inspect**, which opens its record and install below); phones keep the list. Runs are the hive's combs, and Workflows opens from a hive's More editing, on that hive (a live hive's, or the resting hive's), with any draft left from another hive dropped so Save cannot write to it.

## A creature's panel

Selecting a creature opens its panel, in three tabs (`TechnicalInspector`, `CreatureTab`); the breadcrumb ends on the open one (Hive › a hive › a creature › Mind). The panel opens with the creature itself, large, as it looks in the hive, its name and the three tabs beside it (mockup H4). Under them, one line gives its state, its source (when it has one) and its place in the plan, such as "Running · Claude · 3 of 5" (`creatureHeaderLine`, `planPlace`):

- **Needs:** its needs, and what it waits on you for: a plan or task to approve, an escalation, a nested gate, a spent token budget (with **Raise its budget**, which opens its Mind), and a question its agent asked you (drawn from the host catalog, `Agent-UI.md`). Messages to its agent sit with its Work. This was the Messages page.
- **Care is links (cluster B).** The creature sheet changes nothing in place that has a home elsewhere: **Mind** lists what the creature is and links (**Change its instructions, tools, budget and source in the Roster**, and a line when changes to its instructions are proposed) to its role's form in the Roster (`RosterRequests`, `PlaceLinks.openRole`), where the kept creature picker and the instruction proposals now live; **Raise its budget** opens that role; the Source row on Work is read-only and links to the role; the job on Work is read-only with **Change this job in Edit** (`SwarmEditRequests`, opens the swarm's editor with the creature selected) and **Runs on** opens Compute; **Trusted steps** (under an approval) opens Delegations; an escalation offers **Change its source**.\n- **Work:** when this creature does several tasks of its role (`CreatureRoam.kt`), first **The same creature, other tasks**: a pill for each, opening that task; **Here** marks the one it is at now. Then its job (`HiveJobEditor`, mockup H4), the task fields that lived in the Workflows form editor: its **Objective** in a well (changed text is applied with **Apply**), its **Acceptance criteria** as rows, each removed with its ✕, and **Add a criterion**; then **Approval** (a person approves, or none; tapping switches), **Retries** (how many tries, then what happens: the hive stops, it asks you, or another creature takes over; tapping steps through 1 to 5 tries) and **Runs on** (this device, any paired device or a chosen device; with paired devices, tapping sends it off this device and back, and a person's approval step never leaves it). Each change is saved to the hive's workflow the way Edit saves it (`setTerrariumJob`, `WorkflowLibraryHost.saveAuthored`, which validates it first, so a repository change still needs its approval ahead of it); the running run stays as it was, so the change takes effect from its next run, and the panel says so. Then what it's doing, live: its thinking and tool/progress steps for its current source session, newest last, the latest six shown and older ones collapsed behind **Show N earlier lines**; then the field for messages to its agent, apart from that activity; then its state, executor and source, progress, what it was sent, and its artifacts, each opening on its contents. Once its run has completed, failed or been cancelled, **Run again from here** (`RerunFromTaskPanel`) starts a new run in the same lineage where this creature and everything after it work again and the work before it keeps its results; the finished run stays as it was. It asks what the creature should work on, prefilled with its objective, to change it first. When the creature made text and something follows it, **Change what it made** instead keeps the creature's place with the keeper's text as its work and runs only what follows. See `docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE.md`, Running again from a task.
- **Names, counts and sentences that point somewhere are links (cluster D).** Anything that shows something the keeper can change opens the one place it changes (`PlaceLinks`, `LinkText`; a screen with no links draws plain text, never a dead link). A Messages row has **Open this creature**, and a spent budget's button goes straight to the role in the Roster; a Delegations row's name opens its role, its source opens the source; a schedule card has **Open its hive**; the Improvement standing line and its missing-cycle line open Schedules; a role in the Roster lists the hives it works in, each opening that hive (`roleHives`); a Nanny finding's scope and subject open where that is changed (`nannyScopePlace`: models in Connections, roles in the Roster, swarms in the library, memory); the repository crumb opens Connections; a swarm draft that cannot start offers **Switch creatures on in the Roster** or **Link a source in Connections**; a swarm refused for deletion because it came from a package offers **Open the Store**; the Budget meter on Needs is underlined and a full-size target. Sentences that named a place in prose ("set one up from any swarm", "Remove the package in the Store") lost the pointer or gained the link. Not done: the Nanny solution's target, Compute rows, Stable rows, Memory's "Resume under Technical", the terrarium badges, project headers.
- **Time allowed (queen deadlines).** In **Edit**, a step's job row has **Time allowed** (Auto, 5, 15, 30, 60 minutes or Off) beside **Retries** and **Runs on**; the queen sets Auto, and a creature that is already working can take a different time allowed without any other change. The creature's Work tab only shows the job read-only. See Time allowed below.

### A creature's source

An agent creature's Work tab has a **Source** row under its job (`CreatureProviderRow`): **Auto** (the default), or one connected source. It reads and writes its role's `preferredProviderId` through the same save path as the Mind (`onSaveRoleCollection`), using the picker the Roster and the Sources screens use (`ProviderChoiceRow`), so the role is the single place a source is pinned. Because the source belongs to the role, every creature wearing that role follows it, and the row says so. A creature pinned to an A2A orphan shows that pin by the orphan's name and can be set back to Auto. A task that requires a source by id (`ProviderConstraints.RequireProvider`) is also shown as pinned, but only its task can change that, so the row shows the note without the picker. Creatures that are not agents (scripts, tests, repository steps, a person's approval) have no source and no row.

In the terrarium each agent creature wears a small badge at its head: **Pinned: Claude** (Yellow) or **Auto** (Ink), the same `isProviderAuto` rule the engine fails over by. Pinned creatures never switch sources silently: if the source fails the creature stops and says how to switch. Auto creatures fail over to another connected source (docs/Orchestration_layer.md). Source routing never touches approval: `ApprovalPolicy`, `HumanApproval` before mutating repository operations and the validator's ancestor rule are the task's, and neither the row nor the badge reads or writes them.

Drafting a hive (`SwarmDraft`) assigns no sources: it gives each step a `roleId` from the swarm and nothing else, so every drafted creature takes whatever its role already says (Auto unless the keeper pinned that role). That is safe, so it stays; a keeper pins a drafted creature afterwards from its panel.

A creature opens on Needs when it waits on you, otherwise on Work; the open tab survives a restart while the same creature stays open. Waiting on you means a plan or task to approve, an escalation, a nested gate, a spent token budget, or a question from its agent, one definition for the badge, the panel and the hive's Owner attention card (shown only then, opening that creature). The header's Messages badge counts the waits not yet seen and opens the Messages, which lists every kind of wait the badge counts (on a phone too), each leading to where it is resolved; opening a creature's panel, or the Messages, marks waits seen. Only the Nanny's pause and a spend cap stop, which speak for the whole hive, keep a place of their own (`Inbox`).

The panel's Needs holds only decisions; messages to its agent sit with its Work.

The activity comes from `SessionActivityFeeds` (`workflow/SessionActivityFeeds.kt`): the source-backed session gateway records each session's `AgentEvent.Thinking` and non-blank `AgentEvent.Progress` there, keyed by task run and source run (a retry starts a fresh feed). It is in memory only and bounded (300 lines a session, 32 sessions, 600 characters a line, each dated for the Log tab); it is never written into workflow state or the event journal (memory banks thinking on its own) and never sent to the duplex peer (`architecture/DUPLEX_MCP.md`). Messages to and from the agent never enter it. A creature's panel lists everything it did under **Activity**: every attempt it made (a retry starts a new one, labelled **Attempt n**), oldest first, each line dated (UTC) and selectable; nothing collapses. On the hive, under **Jump to active**, one short line reads what the active creature is doing now: its newest thought or step, then the creatures waiting on its output, named by role ("· feeds Implementation Engineer"). Reject plan and Stop workflow stay quiet; the confirmation is where red appears (`BRANDING.md`).

## First launch

Every launch opens on the hive, not on a form or the Keeper's room (`MindMapRunScreen`, `FirstCreature`): one field, **What do you want to get done?**, with **Start**. The field starts with what you fed last on this device, else **Teach me how to use the Aive** (a fixed seed; no model is asked). Starting keeps the first starter (the starters are chosen in the Stable) and names the hive from the first words it was fed (never from an empty tap). **More** holds its name and a repository. One muted line says a free source is picked for you and that free sources may log what you write, with **Use my own key** beside it; the first free text source is connected on its own while none is. Until there is a hive, its places' row is hidden.

Feed it is gated on what the connected agents can reach (`ProviderReach`, `FeedGate`), and the gate sits right above it with its resolution in place:

- **No AI source** (a fresh install has the keyless gateways already on; this is the state after the keyless switch is turned off): on the very first launch the first free text gateway is connected for you and the muted line above replaces the pills; elsewhere, the free keyless gateways, each a one-tap pill (**Use Kilo Gateway · free, no key**), with the one line that prompts and repository text sent to a keyless source leave the device and may be logged; then a quieter **Use your own key** pill to the Keeper's room. Feed it waits quietly.
- **Every agent works only inside a repository** (OpenCode on GitHub Actions, linked with a GitHub token): the repository fields open above Feed it, offering only the kinds those agents work in, alongside the free gateways for work with no repository.
- **A repository no agent works in:** the fields open there too, to choose another kind, or none.

**The plan is reviewed before the run exists** (`orchestration/PlanReview.kt`, `PlanDecisionOverlay.kt`). When a planner is linked, the plan it drafts is read by the **Adversarial Reviewer** (or, with that role off, the first enabled role that may reject and approve plans). It approves, or sends the plan back with its objections and the planner reworks it, for at most **three reviews**; the launch shows it on a small field of its own (the planner's egg growing, the reviewer hatching from it, then the egg of the plan's first step, gestated by both, which cannot hatch until you start the run; docs/Node-Creatures.md, Planning), with the round it is on. Then you get the final say: **The plan, reviewed** (under that field) shows the plan it ended with (each step, its role, and whether it needs your approval), whether the reviewer approved (and after how many rounds) or never did, and everything the reviewer said, round by round, as plain selectable text. **Start the run** creates the run from that plan; **Do not start** creates nothing. You see it whether the reviewer approved or not, and a plan from a remote request is held for you the same way: only you decide it, in the app. The reviewer is advisory: it decides no approval gate and changes nothing, and a reworked plan passes the same validation as any plan. If it cannot be reached (or the planner cannot rework), the loop stops, the plan so far comes to you with the reason, and the launch is never blocked. The reviewer sees the plan and the objective, goes only to a source you have linked, and never moves off a source its role is pinned to. The same review runs on a description drafted into a hive in Edit (**Draft**), where what it said arrives as the draft's notes. **A saved hive with an Orchestrator in it is a rough design** (`SavedHivePlanning.kt`): you drew what you want done and the Orchestrator still decides exactly how best to do it. Launched from the hive's **Launch**, the Orchestrator plans from that design (its other tasks are shown to the planner as the plan so far; it may keep, change, add or remove steps), the reviewer reads the plan, and you decide, exactly as above, on the same field. The run is of the planned hive, saved under a new id: the hive you drew is left as drawn, and a step that kept a task's id keeps everything else you set on it (retries, escalation, source, environment). A saved hive is planned only when everything in it besides the Orchestrator is an agent's task that always runs and needs no approval or the keeper's; one with a script, an action, a repository change, a test run, a task that waits on an outcome or a role's approval launches as it always did, as does any hive with no Orchestrator, and so does a launch with no planner, reviewer or keeper to decide (a remote interface is none of these). A decision left waiting is kept across a restart for a saved hive too, and dropped if the hive or anything else the plan was made from has changed. The starter workflow is not reviewed. A decision you have not made is kept across a restart (`ReviewedPlanStore`, on this device, outside workflow persistence and every export): the next launch of the app offers the same plan and reasons again if nothing it was made from has changed (the objective, repository, runs it continues, the roles the planner may use and the reviewer, compared by a SHA-256 over all of them), and drops it if anything differs or a week has passed. Resuming is not reusing: every new launch, even of the same objective, is planned, reviewed and decided afresh, and the store records no approval, only the decision you were looking at. Run-time gates are asked on every run as before; when a run is rewound (**Run again from here**), the tasks it keeps stay done and what re-runs is gated afresh, with repository changes always. Spend: these calls count against no cap (nothing exists yet to charge them to), which the three-review limit bounds.

Pressing a gated Feed it shows the gate's message and starts nothing. A launch that fails stays beside Feed it, the objective still typed, until the next edit or launch.

## Budgets and spend caps

Three circuit breakers stop spending; none guesses at what a source has not reported (`SpendCircuitBreakers.kt`). A role's token budget does set aside what its running tasks are expected to spend (their input) so that tasks of one role do not all start together; it counts the tokens reported once they land.

- **A role's token budget** (its Mind, per run): once the tokens reported for every task of that role in the run reach it, its running sessions are cancelled and its tasks parked (`ROLE_BUDGET_SPENT`), a stopped one as a fresh attempt, and its unstarted tasks do not start. Each waits on you, on its Needs. Raising or clearing the budget releases them at the next cycle; the current role wins over the run's snapshot.
- **A cost cap per run** and **a spend cap per hour across runs** (This device, in US dollars, blank for none; kept on the device, `SpendCapStore`): when the run's known cost, or the known cost reported on this device in the last hour, reaches its cap, every live session is cancelled and the run is stopped (`WorkflowRun.spendPause`, tasks parked as `SPEND_CAP_STOP`). The Messages shows the stop and **Lift the stop**, which puts every task back where it was; a cancelled session starts again as a new attempt. So raise or clear the cap first: a cap still reached stops the run again (the run cost at its next cycle, the hourly spend when it is next summed: when the run spends, or after a minute).

**Where the dollars come from.** A hosted API reports tokens, not dollars, so the app prices them from one list, `ModelPriceTable` (`providers/ModelPricing.kt`): per million input, output and cached input tokens, as a source's own price page gives them. The list is dated (`ModelPriceTable.CHECKED`, 2026-11-09) and **wrong the day a source changes a price**; check it before trusting a dollar cap with it. It holds six models, each at the **highest** standard rate the source's page gives (its long-context tier, its peak hours, its price after a dated change), so a cap trips early and never late: it is an upper bound, not a quote. Per million input / output tokens: Claude Sonnet 5 and 5.5 $2 / $10 (platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), `gpt-6-astra` $20 / $75 (developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing), `gemini-3.8-flash` $1.50 / $7.50 (aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing), `grok-4.6` $4 / $12 (docs.x.ai/docs/models), `deepseek-flash` $0.30 / $1.20 (api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing), all fetched on that date. Input the source reports as served from its cache is billed at that tier's cache-hit rate; input with no cache split is billed whole at the input rate. Claude's cache-write surcharge is not modelled, so for a prompt that writes the cache the Claude figures run a little low. Every other model is unpriced, among them OpenAI's `gpt-5.6` default (the page prices `gpt-5.6-sol`, `-terra` and `-luna`, which are not aliases of it), `gpt-oss-120b`, Cohere's `command-a-plus-05-2026` and Mistral's `mistral-small-latest`: usage on a model with no price is billed but cannot be counted, so a dollar cap **stops the run that used it** (the stop says "no known price") rather than counting it as free. **Lift the stop** accepts that for this run (known cost still counts toward the caps); a creature's token budget is the limit that does not depend on a price. An anonymous keyless endpoint is free and a CLI agent or the on-device model reports its own cost, so none of them stops a run. Generations made outside any run (the planner, the plan reviewer, the memory clerks) count toward the hourly cap in this process only (`AmbientSpendLedger`); no role budget sees them. Usage a stopped, cancelled or failed session reported but the cycle had not yet read, and a reply that was cut off at the limit, still count.

## Time allowed

The queen sets a reasonable wait for each creature (`workflow/DeadlinePolicy.kt`, no model, no new screen). **Auto** is the default and what every older hive reads as: when an attempt starts she works the figure out from what the creature does (a model answer, a tool loop, a command-line agent, a remote agent, a repository step, a test run), how much it was sent, whether its Source is a shared keyless one, and, once there are enough finished attempts, how long this app's own took (the 90th percentile times one and a half, blended in by count and kept between half and triple the plain figure). The **Time allowed** row of a creature's job (Auto, 5, 15, 30, 60, Off) is the keeper's own figure and wins over hers; **Off** means no limit. A person's approval step and a nested workflow are never timed.

Only the time the creature spends working counts (`TaskClock`, kept on the task, written when something changes and at most once a minute). Time it waits on you (an approval, a question, an escalation, a spent role budget), a spend stop or the Nanny's freeze, and the time the app was closed, are left out; the keyless pacer's wait before it starts is not counted, a slow or rate-limited Source is.

At the allowance nothing is stopped: the creature's panel says **Taking longer than expected (allowed N min)**, and at Helpful the queen says so in Messages with a link to its Work tab, where **Time allowed** can be raised at once, even while it runs (a live edit takes the time allowed of a creature already working, nothing else of it). At twice the allowance (or five minutes past it), or past the allowance with a Source that spoke and then went silent, the creature is stopped: its provider session or executor run is cancelled, what it spent is still counted (a spend cap still wins), and it fails with "Timed out after N minutes of work", which the existing failover and retry read as a timeout. After two such stops the creature goes to you as the normal Escalation (Retry task, Stop workflow) whatever its escalation policy says; your Retry starts the count over. The Story shows the usual retry and escalation rows. Switch: `HARD_DEADLINES_ENABLED`.

## Pause, change, resume

A live hive's row has **Pause** beside Stop. Paused, nothing new starts (no creature, no system step, no remote compute, and a retry waits); what is already running finishes; no session is cancelled and no task is parked, so **Resume** starts nothing over. The pill reads **Paused · Resume** and the hive says it is paused. Approvals and decisions still go through. It is the run's `keeperPause` (`WorkflowKeeperPause`), kept with the run, so it survives a restart; a spend cap's stop and the Nanny's pause still take effect over it (`ApplicationRuntime.setKeeperPause`, `SpendCircuitBreakerTest`).

Change, with or without a pause: a creature's job and criteria saved from its panel reach the live run when that creature has not started (waiting, ready or about to retry), because the engine reads a task's definition when it starts it (`ApplicationRuntime.applyLiveDefinitionEdit`). A creature that is running or done, a creature's role, executor, condition and policies, and the hive's shape change from the next run: the saved hive keeps the edit, and the run's own copy is never written over it (`WorkflowRuntimeCoordinator.persist`, `LiveDefinitionEdit`, `ApplicationRuntimeLiveEditTest`). A change that would leave the run's graph invalid (creatures waiting on each other) is not applied to the run, and the app log says why. Before this, a save was written over within a second by the runtime's own copy.

**Rewind:** on a live run a creature that has started or finished offers **Rewind to here**: it and everything downstream of it (and the approvals a repository change needs) start again as a new attempt, while the rest of the swarm keeps running. Only the sessions inside that set are cancelled, and so are the remote runs of its system steps (a GitHub Actions run, a nested workflow, an external service call), so the new attempt does not run beside the old; what the creature spent stays on its record; its old approval gates are closed and its old attempt and artifacts stay as history (`WorkflowRuntimeCoordinator.rewind`, `ApplicationRuntime.rewindToTask`; the app log says it). **Run again from here** still forks a new run (it stops the old one, but only once the request is accepted: a refused one leaves the live run running), and is the way to run a finished run again. Open decision: a rewound creature's downstream creatures that had already finished are run again too, because they consumed the old output; that is wrong if you wanted to keep them, and then Run again from here with an edited output is the way. Pause is wrong if you meant "park everything now": that is Stop, or a spend cap. Structural edits (adding a creature, rewiring) to a running hive are saved but reach the run only from the next run; a run records the hive it started with (and the job and criteria edits it took), so if the app restarts first it still resumes against that, never against the later save. A cancel or stop that a source rejects or fails is written to the run's log as a failed cancel and asked again on the next cycles (up to five times), not dropped. Opening another project, or a project with no run, while a run is live cancels that run, as a new launch does: only the run on screen is cycled and held to your spend caps, so one left behind would spend unwatched.

## The hive's story

- **The flowchart is the back of each creature in Edit (cluster G; it was a third view beside Live and Edit).** In Edit a creature is drawn as itself, with no card around it. Tap it and it turns over (a `rotationY` flip with camera distance, about 360 ms, the creature face gone past 90 degrees) to the step's card, as the Flowchart drew it: the name, the role (or what kind of executor does it) and the state in words, on the state's colour (working yellow, complete green, waiting ink, waiting on you or failed red; a step a condition skipped is dimmed, not red), the selected card with the strong border (`FlowCardFace`, built from `WorkflowFlowchart.of(definition, run)`, `WorkflowFlowchartTest`). The card is invisible until flipped. With Reduce motion (`AzphaltMotion`) the turn is a 90 ms crossfade. **Only one creature is flipped at a time** (tapping another turns the first back), and the flip is a pure reducer (`CardFlip.kt`, `reduceCardFlip`, `CardFlipTest`): Front or Card, by Tap, OtherNodeTapped, SwipeAny, SheetExpanded, SheetPeeked and Back, giving the sheet's detent (none, peek, expanded). Tapping the card again, Back, Escape, or a swipe across it up, down, left or right **while the sheet is still at its peek** turns it back and dismisses the sheet; once the keeper has dragged the sheet up, drags belong to the sheet. A flipped card is not moved by dragging. A screen reader finds the creature on the front and the card (its step, role and state, selected) on the back, and a **Flip** action on both. **The sheet:** on a phone a tap in Edit shows the creature's bottom sheet at its **peek** (partially expanded, `CardFlipPeekHeight`, not full height, not modal so the card stays tappable), leading with the card's detail and growing, when dragged up, into the creature's own sheet with its tabs. On a wide screen there is no sheet: the docked creature panel stays the host and leads with the same detail. Live is unchanged: a tap opens the creature's modal sheet. **The card's detail** (`FlowchartCardDetail.kt`, `flowchartCardDetail`, `FlowchartCardDetailTest`; `FlowchartCardDetailPanel`): one plain sentence (what is active, or what holds it) and one button to where it is unblocked, using the shell's own links (`FlowchartLinks`: `openInbox`, `onOpenLimits`, `onOpenCompute`, `openConnectionsFromPanel`, `onReconfigureProvider`, `openCreature`), never a picker of its own. Active (Planning, Running, Verifying, Retrying): "Working: <its progress message, else its role>", **Open creature**. An approval gate, a question the agent asked, or an escalation: **Open messages** (that creature's Needs). `SPEND_CAP_STOP`: **Open spend limits**. `ROLE_BUDGET_SPENT`: **Raise its budget** (that creature's Mind). `remote_compute_unavailable`: **Open Compute** (the creature, where Compute is not offered). An unavailable executor integration, or no connected source: **Fix in Connections**. A failure where the source rejected its key, ran out of credit or answered with an HTTP 4xx: **Fix in Connections** for that source (the credential dialog). The Nanny's pause: **Open messages**. Waiting on dependencies: **Show <step>**, which flips to the step that is itself stuck, else the first unfinished. Any other failure or hold, and a finished, skipped or unstarted step: **Open creature**; Open creature, Open messages and Raise its budget expand the sheet to the creature's tab (`creatureSheetAllowed`: Live opens the modal sheet, Edit only while a card is flipped). A hive at rest and the Workflows editor's bowl have no shell to host a sheet: the detail sits under the field. **The dependency cue** is the terrarium's own: the strand between creatures (the clasp and its pulse, green for what waits on success, red on failure, yellow on either) shows the order and the condition that the Flowchart's arrows and labels showed; its arrowheads and "if it fails" words are not drawn. A saved Flowchart view (`hive.view.flow`) opens Edit once and is cleared (`migratedToEdit`). Open decisions: a failure is led to Connections only when its words say key, credit, or HTTP 4xx (wrong if a source reports its refusal in other words: it then leads to the creature, which shows the text); the spend stop leads to the caps, where the lift is one step further in the Messages; the flip applies only in Edit (wrong if the keeper meant Live creatures to flip too); a hive at rest or one with no run shows the card's detail only under the field or in the docked panel.

A live hive's **Story** pill swaps the hive for its run's story, in several views (the chosen one is remembered, `overview.timeline.view`). **Overview**, the way back to the hive, **Waterfall**, **Events**, **Memory** and **Log** share one row (`AzphaltTabs(leading = …, trailing = …)`); Memory shows the Memory swarm screen in place, not a view of the story, and Log, last, is described below. The row stays fixed under the heading while the view below it scrolls, and the row scrolls the selected view into sight:

- **Waterfall** (the default): one row per creature, one bar per attempt, on a shared time axis, so work running in parallel overlaps. Each bar is coloured by phase: Planning, Waiting on you (drawn as a thin pause line: a gate), Running, Verifying, Retrying. A white tick ends a finished attempt, a red one a failed attempt; a bar still going runs to now. Hovering or tapping a bar shows its phases and usage (tokens, cost); tapping also selects its creature. Each bar is one labelled button for screen readers. The bars unroll once on arrival and, while the run lives, grow once a second; with reduced motion they do neither and move only when new events arrive.
- **Events:** the run's events in order.
- **Log** (`RunLogView`, `log/`): the full log, one source at a time, picked from a second row of tabs (remembered, `overview.timeline.log.source`). Oldest line first, newest at the foot; the list follows the foot only while you are there. Each line is its UTC time, whose it is (the task, and the role doing it), and the text, plain and selectable, never rendered as markup. For the sources about the run's steps (All activity, Agent actions, Run log) a third row of tabs filters by **role**: **All roles**, then each role that acts in what is shown, in the order they first act. The five sources, in this order of tabs (All activity, Agent actions, Run log, Memory log, App log; the memory log is absent where the app has no memory layer):
  - **All activity:** the run's events and what its agents thought and did, interleaved by time (events first when the time is the same), each line keeping its role. Nothing is dropped or reworded: it is the two sources below in one list (`allActivityLines`). Agent lines are in memory, this launch only.
  - **Run log:** every event of the run, in time order, with the whole reason (Events cuts reasons to 80 characters). From the durable event journal, so it survives a restart. Like Events, it reads the active run (`onLoadRunTimeline`).
  - **Agent actions:** everything this run's agents thought and did, interleaved by time, from `SessionActivityFeeds`: each line is now dated, and a session keeps its newest 300 lines (was 60). Still in memory only, this launch only, never exported.
  - **App log:** the app's own diagnostic lines (`appLog`, `AppLog`): launch refusals and failures, settings failures, store-step failures with their stack traces. The newest 500, dated, kept on this device across restarts (`aive.app-log.v1`), keys scrubbed before they are kept (`ControlRedaction.scrub`).
  - **Memory log:** what consolidation did, one line per packet it applied, completed, declined or failed, with the workflow it was for (`MemoryLayerController.journal`). In memory only, the newest 500, this launch only: nothing durable records when a queue entry finished. The source is absent where the app has no memory layer.
  Log text is the record's own words, not translated.

A creature's Work reaches it too: **Open its trace** turns the Story on and closes the panel, leaving that creature selected on it.

The waterfall is a pure function of the run's event journal (`runWaterfall` in `RunWaterfall.kt`, tested in `RunWaterfallTest`):

~~~
TaskStarted                         -> an attempt begins (Running)
events before it starts             -> it begins early, in Planning
ApprovalRequired, HumanDecisionRequired -> Waiting on you until the decision
work before an attempt's first gate -> Planning (a plan gate follows planning)
ArtifactCreated                     -> Verifying
VerificationFailed, RetryScheduled  -> Retrying, until the next attempt starts
TaskCompleted / TaskFailed / TaskCancelled -> it ends
the run's end                       -> every open bar ends; otherwise they run to now
ProviderUsageRecorded               -> added to the attempt it fell in
~~~

## Removing a creature and deleting a swarm

**Remove** (Edit, with a creature selected, beside Duplicate; with Cluster on, **Remove N creatures**) takes the creature's task out of the swarm together with every strand touching it, as feeder or as fed. A knot that named it goes back to the plain one. It is one step on the Undo history, so one Undo brings back the creatures and their strands. Strands that ran through a removed creature are not bridged: what it fed is left without that feeder, and you wire it again if you want. An edit that would break the swarm is refused and nothing changes; taking out the approval ahead of a repository change is one. Removing the last creature leaves an empty swarm. While a run of the swarm is live, Remove is not offered and the page says why.

**Swarm options** (`HiveActionsMenu.kt`): a long press on any swarm in the district opens a menu naming it, with **Open** (what a tap does), **Delete swarm** and **Close**; a screen reader finds it as the swarm's "Swarm options" action. Choosing Delete swarm does what the red pill below does, asked from here: it looks first and says in a plain line why it cannot (and deletes nothing), or asks, naming the swarm, and the district is read again once the swarm is gone.

**Delete swarm** (a red pill under the wiring of a swarm at rest) asks first, naming the swarm, and says it cannot be undone. It deletes only that swarm from this device. It is refused, with a plain reason and nothing deleted, while a run of it is live, when another swarm uses it as a circuit, and when it came from a package (remove the package in the Store). Past runs are kept: they stay in history and open read-only under the title "Deleted swarm", showing the tasks the run recorded, and can be viewed but not resumed or re-run. Startup skips a latest run whose swarm is gone and starts empty. Your memory, repositories, runs and other swarms are never touched, and no repository operation is involved. Afterwards the app returns to the district.

## Zoom levels

The hive's camera follows the selection instead of asking you to zoom and pan (`TerrariumFocus.kt`, `TerrariumCamera`; Edit and Live). By default it shows the **Whole swarm**. Selecting a creature (or grouping a cluster, which counts as one focus) starts a stack of views, innermost first, each fitted with padding and animated (instant with reduced motion). A view that is empty, or that shows the same creatures as the one before it, is skipped:

1. **Creature**: the creature with a little context.
2. **Neighbours**: it, the creatures feeding it and those it feeds.
3. **Path to the orchestrator** or **Path to the end goal**, whichever is shorter (a tie goes to the orchestrator). The orchestrator is the creature with the Orchestrator role, else the entry creature (nothing feeds it); the end goal is the nearest creature nothing else waits on.
4. The other of those two paths.
5. **Path to the active step**: the shortest path over strands to the nearest running creature (Live only).
6. **Path to the blocks**: the shortest path to the nearest creature waiting for approval, blocked or failed.
7. **Whole chain**: all its ancestors and descendants.
8. **Whole swarm**.

**−** and the system Back step one view out; **+** steps back in through the views already passed. Selecting another creature starts again at Creature; tapping bare ground lets go of the selection and shows the whole hive. Creatures outside the view are dimmed, and the strands of its path are drawn in Yellow. A label by the control names the view (tap it to snap back to it). Pinching or dragging the ground moves the camera freely ("Free view") without changing the stack; stepping, or tapping the label, returns to the view. Drag empty ground to pan, pinch (or two fingers) to zoom and pan. A strand is cut by dragging across it (a drag that starts on a strand); a strand is drawn from a creature's tail.

## The queen's suggestions (mid-run)

While a swarm runs the queen may offer a suggestion as a row of Messages (`QueenNote.suggestion`, the **From the queen** row titled **A suggestion from the queen**), with **Go ahead** (when she can apply it herself) and **No thanks**, plus a link to the place where you would change the thing. No new screen, tab or dialog (the one question, before a redo, is the existing confirm dialog). Nothing is applied without your tap in the app: no remote interface, scripted call or delegation reaches it (`QueenSuggestionInvariantsTest`).

**Where they are kept.** Each is a row of the one improvement ledger with source **Queen** (`ImprovementSource.Queen`; for her rows the cycle id is the run, `sourceRef` is the swarm, `target` is the step or `swarm`), so the compare-and-set decision, retention and "dismissed is remembered" are the ledger's own. They are per run, capped at 100 (oldest first, never touching another source), and the Keeper's room Improvement list does not show them. Her rows are read by the cycle at most every five seconds (`ApplicationRuntime.queenSuggestLocked`; it never awaits a model and never stops the cycle); an older app that meets a ledger holding `Queen` rows cannot read it (forward-only).

**What she can offer** (`QueenAction`, a closed menu; signals are counted from the run, `queenSignals`, plus the run's cost): **Redo from step** (a step that builds on a finished one has failed twice: she redoes the finished step and what follows; asks first); **Source** (a step moved to another Source: link to its Mind, applies from the next run); **Raise its budget** (a role at 80 percent of its token budget with steps left: link to its Mind); **Raise the allowance** (the known cost is 70 percent of the run's allowance with at least 40 percent of the steps left: link to the Keeper's room); **Rest** (only while the Nanny's report is waiting on its reviewer: your own pause); **Reword a step** (a step that has not started takes a new job through the live edit; built and tested, but nothing proposes wording until the model checkpoints exist). Never offered: removing or loosening an approval, moving work to a Source that can write to the repository, skipping a step, touching the Nanny. She makes her own judgement of whether a swarm is working, with or without a Nanny. The Nanny does something different: she makes sure it works as well as possible, and her findings are relayed in her words and quoted.

**Pacing** (`QueenPacing`): one open at **Quiet**, two at **Helpful**; fifteen minutes between new ones; five a run; one per step and action; a dismissed one is not offered again for the same step and action, in any run, for 14 days. One whose step started, or whose signal has passed, becomes Superseded and leaves Messages. While the swarm is frozen or stopped nothing is offered or applied and Messages shows only that page.

**Accepting.** The tap re-checks everything (`queenAcceptRefused`): the run is under way, the step has not started (reword), the swarm is not already resting. It then uses the call you already have: the live edit for a reword, the rewind for a redo, your pause for a rest, and only if that took does the row become Adopted; a second tap, or a suggestion whose step has since started, changes nothing and says "That suggestion no longer applies". A reword is refused for a step that can change a repository (a role that writes, a script, a service) unless it sits behind a human approval.

**Content from outside.** If the run's objective or the step's inputs came from outside your own typing, the row says **Based on content from outside. Look before you accept.**, a redo asks first (it always does), and a new job for a step is not offered or applied.

**Badge.** Each open suggestion counts once toward the Messages badge and is read like any other item; the queen's other notes never count.
